# Nubank — wedge-narrowing from "Brazilian banking" to "credit cards"

David Vélez had personal Brazilian banking pain and a Sequoia background. The broad hypothesis — "disrupt Brazilian banking" — passed Q1's right-to-exist test on founder-market fit and macro-category dynamics, but didn't yet identify the wedge that would resolve Q2.

The pressure-test process (per Sequoia's account of the Arc framework applied to Nubank) was wedge-narrowing: which specific financial-services pain had rabid demand from a specific cohort? The answer wasn't deposits, wasn't lending, wasn't a full neobank — it was **credit cards specifically**. Brazilian credit cards from incumbent banks (Itaú, Bradesco, Santander) had hostile UX, hostile customer service, and hostile fee structures. The cohort that would defect was identifiable: urban, smartphone-native Brazilians with existing credit cards they hated.

The Q1 wedge ("credit cards, not full banking") plus the Q2 rabid-demand cohort enabled Nubank to launch a single product (a no-fee credit card) and capture the cohort's defection. The "marry the idea for a decade-plus" statement was not "be a Brazilian bank" — it was something closer to "give Brazilians financial products that don't suck, starting with credit cards."

## What the founder did

- Refused the broad "Brazilian banking disruption" framing as the Q1 articulation.
- Identified credit cards specifically as the Q2 wedge through prospect pressure-testing.
- Built a single-product v1 (no-fee credit card) instead of a full neobank stack.
- Used Brazilian regulatory-arbitrage timing window as the unfair advantage (Vélez had insider visibility into the regulatory calendar via Sequoia).

## What they refused

- Refused to launch deposits, loans, or full retail banking in v1.
- Refused expansion outside Brazil in the first 3 years.
- Refused to compete on broad "fintech innovation" — competed on credit-card UX specifically.

## How this applies to your situation

If you're at Q1 and your articulation is broad ("disrupt X industry"), this is the Nubank pattern: the broad articulation passes founder-market fit and category but doesn't yet name the wedge. Pressure-test by listing every specific pain inside the broad space and finding the one cohort with rabid defection-ready demand. The marry-the-idea statement should be wedge-specific, not industry-broad. "Disrupt Brazilian banking" is a thesis. "Give Brazilians credit cards that don't suck" is a Q1 resolution.

Route back to: `arc-q1-right-to-exist` or `arc-q2-do-people-care`, depending on which question is in flight.
