# Square — Q4 resolution through pricing-model iteration

Sequoia article 2 cites Square as a case where the founders iterated the pricing model en route to Q4 resolution. The exact timeline is thin in the cited source; public reporting (Square S-1, founder interviews) indicates Square's pricing was 2.75% per swipe from launch (2010) with the reader given free as a customer-acquisition mechanism. The "hardware sales → transaction fees" narrative often attributed to Square in startup folklore appears to overstate the timeline — the transaction-fee model was the original commercial pricing.

What Square did clearly iterate on:

- **Pricing transparency:** Flat 2.75% vs. incumbent processors' opaque tiered pricing (the category reframe). This is the durable Q4 lesson.
- **Pricing power:** Resisted pressure to add complexity. Single-rate pricing became part of the brand promise.
- **Unit economics at scale:** Free reader as CAC, transaction fees as LTV. The $500M revenue path required volume, not margin per transaction — different math than incumbent processor model.

The Q4 lesson generalizes: pricing-model fit is part of Q4, not separable from it. Revenue is not Q4 resolution. The Q4 criterion (Sequoia, verbatim) is "paying customers who feel they get great value" *and* "chart a path to $500M in revenue." Revenue without positive unit economics at scale doesn't clear the bar.

## What the founder did

- Modeled unit economics on transaction-fee pricing from day one.
- Positioned flat-rate pricing as a category reframe against incumbent tiered processor pricing.
- Used free hardware as customer acquisition rather than a primary revenue line.
- Defended single-rate pricing against industry pressure to add complexity.

## What they refused

- Refused complex tiered pricing despite incumbent industry norms.
- Refused to compete on hardware features (the reader was iconic, but the moat was the pricing model + category positioning).
- Refused to add per-transaction fees on top of the flat rate.

## How this applies to your situation

If you're at Q4 with revenue but weak unit economics, Square is the case to match against. The lesson: revenue is not Q4 resolution. The Q4 criterion (Sequoia, verbatim) is "paying customers who feel they get great value" *and* "chart a path to $500M in revenue." Revenue without unit-economics positivity at scale doesn't clear the bar.

The pricing-pivot pattern is common at Q4: the v1 pricing model often caps the TAM. The Q4 work is modeling the alternative pricing and verifying it produces the $500M path with positive unit economics. Operational momentum on the v1 pricing is irrelevant if the model can't get to Q4.
